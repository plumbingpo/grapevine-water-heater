Keeping Your Arlington Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Arlington

Long before a tank floods a Arlington garage, it usually shows warning signs. Pay attention to a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.

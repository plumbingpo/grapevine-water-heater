Someone wants the tank moved from a hall closet to the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines sized for the load, vents routed and pitched properly, circuits matched to the heater, and water lines run cleanly. Grapevine Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Good installations start on paper. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. A heat pump model needs space, a room that stays reasonably warm, and a condensate drain. An electric tankless unit may need a heavy dedicated circuit and enough panel capacity to support it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. For electric and heat pump units, the circuit is matched to the load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. When to Call for Water Heater Installation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot

  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Consider the gas side first. An undersized line starves a new heater whenever the furnace or range runs, and a whole-house tankless unit usually needs more gas than the existing line carries. Vent material, size, pitch, and clearances all have to be right, or exhaust drifts back inside. Electric models need their own math on breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Set the thermostat near 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply

Most gas water heaters in Grapevine, TX work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Grapevine Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
